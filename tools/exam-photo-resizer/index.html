    How to Resize Photos & Signatures to Exact KB Without Portal Rejection

    Indian recruitment portals and scholarship websites run strict automated validators the moment you select a file. Unlike social networks that accept any JPEG and recompress it on their servers, portals run by SSC, UPSC, NTA, RRB, MPSC, and MahaDBT reject files outright if a passport photo is even 200 bytes outside the allowed KB window, if the pixel width or height misses the specification, or if the JPEG header reports the default 72 DPI instead of 200 or 300 DPI.

    Most candidates run into trouble because standard phone photo-editor apps reduce file size blindly without giving control over both the minimum and maximum KB limits. When you compress a clean white-paper signature down to fit under 20 KB, ordinary compressors frequently overshoot and drop the file to 6 KB or 8 KB, triggering an immediate “File size must be between 10 KB and 20 KB” error on SSC OTR or MPSC forms.

    1. Pick your exam preset first: Select your exact examination or document type from the preset dropdown or quick chips above. The tool automatically locks the exact pixel width, pixel height, Min KB floor, Max KB ceiling, required DPI, and portal-safe filename.
    2. Upload directly from your phone gallery or computer: Large 12MP to 50MP smartphone camera shots are automatically pre-downscaled inside browser memory to a 2000 px working buffer so mid-range Android devices never stutter or crash.
    3. Frame and whiten in real time: Drag your image inside the locked aspect-ratio canvas, adjust the zoom slider, or tap Fit Inside White Canvas so no edge of your signature or ears on your passport photo gets clipped. If you photographed your signature under indoor room lighting, slide the Signature Paper Whitener to 55%–80% to convert grey paper shadows into pure #FFFFFF.
    4. Download the verified JPEG: Verify that all five green pre-flight checks pass—format, pixel dimensions, KB window (using strict 1024-byte KB math), stamped DPI, and clean filename—then tap Download Ready JPG.

    SSC Signature Resizer (10KB to 20KB) & UPSC 3-Signature Vertical Rules Explained

    The SSC Signature (10–20 KB) check is notoriously picky because two constraints collide: a crisp black-ink signature on pure white paper compresses extraordinarily well in JPEG, often falling below 9 KB even at 95% quality. When an ordinary online compressor produces an 8.4 KB file, the SSC OTR form rejects it immediately. Conversely, if a candidate shoots the signature on dim, textured paper to inflate the file size, automated OCR verification flags the grey background for poor contrast. Our resizer solves both problems simultaneously: the Signature Paper Whitener cleans the paper to pure white, and if the crisp output still falls below 10 KB, our Minimum KB Floor Protection injects a standard harmless JFIF COM comment segment so the file registers safely between 12 KB and 15 KB without altering a single pixel.

    For UPSC Civil Services, NDA, CDS, and CAPF applications, the commission requires applicants to sign three times vertically on a blank white sheet, scan it inside a square 400 × 400 px canvas at 300 DPI, keep the file size between 20 KB and 100 KB, and name the file strictly signature.jpg (and photo.jpg for the photograph). Selecting the UPSC Signature (3× Vertical Stack) preset enables the 3× vertical duplicator automatically so a single neat signature crop generates a balanced three-tier vertical layout ready for upload.

    How to Merge & Compress Marksheets into a Single PDF Under 256 KB for MahaDBT & College Forms

    Scholarship portals like MahaDBT (Post-Matric, EBC, OBC, and SC/ST schemes) and state engineering/medical CAP counselling rounds frequently require a single combined PDF containing your 10th marksheet, 12th marksheet, Leaving Certificate, Income Certificate, or Caste Validity Certificate—all squeezed strictly under 256 KB (or 300 KB for NTA NEET category/PwD PDFs).

    Taking photos of 3 or 4 A4 sheets on a modern phone easily totals 12 MB to 18 MB. Standard “Print to PDF” tools leave every page at full camera resolution, producing a 6 MB PDF that fails portal upload. In Tab 2 (Multi-Image to Target-KB PDF Maker), our client-side engine iteratively scales down each page onto a clean white A4 canvas and runs a binary search across JPEG quality tiers before embedding the pages into a compact pdf-lib document. It verifies the final .pdf byte size directly in memory so your merged file stays readable and strictly under 256 KB or your chosen cap.

    5 Common Reasons Government & College Portals Reject Uploads (and How to Fix Them)

    • 1. Hidden PNG or WebP extensions renamed to .jpg: Renaming photo.png to photo.jpg in Windows or Android file managers does not change the file’s magic bytes. Portals inspect the header bytes and reject mismatched files. Always re-encode cleanly to true baseline JPEG.
    • 2. Transparent PNG signatures turning solid black: When a digital signature PNG with a transparent alpha channel is converted to JPEG naively, transparent pixels become #000000 black, hiding the ink completely. Our canvas always paints pure #FFFFFF before drawing your image.
    • 3. Spaces, brackets, or special characters in filenames: Filenames like IMG_2026 (1).jpg or WhatsApp Image 2026-11-27.jpeg often crash legacy portal validators. Our presets enforce clean lowercase underscore filenames such as ssc_signature.jpg or photo.jpg.
    • 4. Missing 200 or 300 DPI metadata in JFIF headers: Browser canvas exports do not embed physical print DPI. Patching the JPEG APP0 density bytes ensures portal DPI validators accept the image on the first attempt.
    • 5. Wrong aspect ratio distorting faces or clipping signature strokes: Stretching a 4:3 selfie into a square 1:1 UPSC frame squashes facial proportions and triggers biometric rejection. Use the interactive drag-and-zoom framing box with Fit Inside White Canvas to preserve natural proportions.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    1. Are my passport photos, signatures, or Aadhaar marksheets uploaded to any server? ▾

    No. Every crop, paper-whitening pass, JPEG binary-search compression, JFIF DPI header patch, and PDF merge runs 100% locally inside your browser using HTML5 Canvas and JavaScript. Zero bytes of your personal documents ever leave your device.

    2. How does this tool keep a crisp signature between 10 KB and 20 KB without going too small? ▾

    Government portals like SSC OTR reject files below 10 KB just as strictly as files above 20 KB. First our engine binary-searches JPEG quality to land near the midpoint of your target KB window. If even 98% quality is still smaller than the Min KB floor, it inserts a harmless standard JFIF COM metadata segment so the file sits safely inside the required KB range with zero pixel distortion.

    3. Does the downloaded JPG actually carry 200 DPI, 300 DPI, or 600 DPI metadata? ▾

    Yes. Standard browser canvas exports default to 72 or 96 DPI. After compressing your image, our engine patches bytes 13 to 17 of the JPEG APP0 JFIF header with pixels-per-inch units and the exact big-endian X and Y density (200, 300, or 600 DPI), so Windows File Properties, macOS Preview, and strict portal validators read the required DPI.

    4. How does the UPSC 3× Vertical Signature Stack option work? ▾

    UPSC requires a single 400 × 400 px JPEG (20–100 KB, 300 DPI) showing your signature signed three times vertically one below the other. Simply upload one clear photo of your signature, crop it once in the framing box, and enable the UPSC 3× Vertical Signature Stack toggle to duplicate it cleanly three times vertically inside the 400 × 400 px output.

    5. Will my 12MP to 50MP smartphone camera photo crash or lag my budget Android phone? ▾

    No. As soon as you select a large smartphone camera photo, the tool automatically pre-downscales it onto an intermediate canvas capped at 2000 px on the longest side while preserving aspect ratio. This prevents mobile browser memory spikes and keeps touch-dragging and slider adjustments smooth.

    6. How do I merge multiple marksheets into one PDF under 256 KB for MahaDBT or NEET? ▾

    Switch to the Multi-Image to Target-KB PDF Maker tab, select up to 10 photos of your marksheets or certificates, arrange their page order with Move Up or Move Down, pick 256 KB (MahaDBT) or 300 KB (NEET) from the Max PDF Size Cap selector, and click Generate & Download PDF. The tool automatically calibrates resolution and JPEG compression before building the A4 PDF so the final .pdf stays strictly under your limit.
