How to Use the Resistor Color Code Decoder

Reading tiny painted stripes on axial through-hole resistors under workbench lighting can easily lead to misread multipliers. This interactive decoder maps the international IEC 60062 color standard directly onto a live SVG diagram so you can confirm both the visual pattern and exact electrical limits in seconds.

  1. Select the band count: Count the colored rings on your physical resistor and choose 4-Band, 5-Band, or 6-Band mode.
  2. Match each band color: Starting from the tightly grouped end of the resistor, pick the color for each digit, multiplier, tolerance, and optional temperature coefficient band.
  3. Verify against your target circuit: Compare the SVG preview with the physical component and inspect the calculated minimum and maximum Ohm tolerance boundaries.

Technical Breakdown: IEC 60062 Band Equations & TCR Specs

Every axial resistor encodes resistance logarithmically using significant digits multiplied by a power of ten. In a 4-band resistor, resistance equals (10 × D1 + D2) × 10^M, where D1 and D2 span 0–9 and M covers multipliers from 0.01 (Silver) up to 10^9 (White).

5-Band & 6-Band Precision Encoding

Precision metal-film resistors use 5 or 6 bands because tighter tolerances (such as ±1% Brown or ±0.1% Violet) require three significant digits: (100 × D1 + 10 × D2 + D3) × 10^M. A 6th band specifies the Temperature Coefficient of Resistance (TCR) in ppm/K. For example, a Brown 6th band (100 ppm/K) shifts resistance by at most 0.01% per degree Celsius, making it ideal for precision op-amp gain dividers and instrumentation amplifiers.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know which end of a resistor to read first?

Start from the end where the color bands are grouped closer together. The tolerance band (often gold or silver) is usually spaced slightly farther apart or sits closer to the opposite lead.

What is the difference between 4-band and 5-band resistors?

4-band resistors provide two significant digits, a multiplier, and a tolerance band. 5-band resistors add a third significant digit for higher precision (±1% or ±0.5% metal film).

What does the 6th band on a precision resistor represent?

The 6th band indicates the Temperature Coefficient of Resistance (TCR) measured in ppm/K, showing how much the resistance drifts per degree Kelvin of temperature change.

What do gold and silver mean when used as multiplier bands?

When placed in the multiplier slot, Gold divides the significant digits by 10 (×0.1 Ω) and Silver divides by 100 (×0.01 Ω), enabling sub-Ohm current-sense resistor values.

Why are resistor values standardized around E12 and E24 series?

Preferred number series space nominal resistor values logarithmically so adjacent tolerances overlap cleanly without redundant manufacturing SKUs.

Should I still verify high-precision resistors with a multimeter?

Yes. Color bands can fade under heat or appear ambiguous under warm lighting. Always confirm critical analog reference or safety resistors with a calibrated digital multimeter.
